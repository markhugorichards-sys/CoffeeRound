# COFFEE ROUND — MASTER WEBSITE, E-COMMERCE, SEO & AGENTIC COMMERCE BUILD SPECIFICATION

## 1. Your role and mission

Act as a combined senior brand strategist, premium consumer-brand designer, e-commerce conversion specialist, full-stack developer, technical SEO architect, AI-search optimisation consultant, product photographer, CRO specialist and UK e-commerce compliance lead.

Build a complete, launch-ready website and online store for **Coffee Round**, a premium ready-to-drink iced coffee brand.

Do not produce merely a homepage mockup, a static design, or a generic Shopify-style template. Build a cohesive, functional, mobile-first brand and commerce experience with a complete content management system, product catalogue, shopping basket, checkout integration, customer communications, analytics, SEO infrastructure and an ongoing growth strategy.

The website must be visually distinctive, commercially credible, accessible, fast, technically robust and easy to operate without a developer for routine updates.

### Business objectives, in priority order

1. Establish Coffee Round as a recognisable premium iced-coffee brand.
2. Convert website visitors into profitable first-time and repeat customers.
3. Encourage multi-can purchases, discovery packs, multipacks and subscriptions where commercially viable.
4. Build organic visibility for relevant commercial and informational searches.
5. Make products, prices, ingredients, availability, shipping and brand information understandable to search engines and AI-powered discovery systems.
6. Enable eligible AI assistants and commerce agents to discover, compare and accurately recommend Coffee Round products.
7. Build an owned customer base through consent-based email marketing, repeat-purchase journeys and customer service.
8. Create an expandable platform that can support new flavours, product formats, retail partnerships and international markets.

**Definition of success:** a fully tested, operational e-commerce website that can accept genuine orders once the owner has supplied and verified all required business, product, payment, shipping and legal information.

Never represent an unfinished feature, payment connection, product claim or external integration as operational.

## 2. Brand identity and art direction

Use the existing Coffee Round brand direction and supplied visual references as the creative starting point.

The design must communicate everyday premium quality: contemporary, natural, calm, indulgent and accessible.

### Core colour palette

Use the following colours consistently across the website, product cards, navigation, buttons, typography, photography treatments and supporting graphics.

- **Signature Olive:** #73764B
- **Oat Cream:** #F3EBDD
- **Warm Sand:** #CDB79D
- **Espresso Brown:** #493024

Use oat cream as the dominant background, olive for primary actions and brand accents, espresso brown for text and contrast, and warm sand for secondary surfaces.

White or a clean neutral may be used when needed for product photography, accessibility or visual contrast.

Do not introduce a rainbow of flavour colours. Use restrained, flavour-specific cues only when they improve product recognition.

### Typography

Choose a highly legible contemporary sans-serif for navigation, product information, buttons and body copy.

A refined editorial serif may be used sparingly for lifestyle headlines or supporting brand storytelling.

Use consistent type scales, generous whitespace, clear hierarchy and restrained letter spacing. Ensure that body text is readable on mobile and that important information is never embedded only in images.

### Logo and graphic language

Use the approved Coffee Round logo if supplied. If it has not been supplied, create a proposed text-based wordmark and a simple botanical leaf symbol as a design concept, subject to owner approval.

The name COFFEE ROUND must be the principal brand identifier.

Do not create an elaborate mascot, multiple competing logos, unnecessary decorative icons or complex illustrations.

### Photography direction

The visual language should include:

- Warm, directional natural sunlight.
- Botanical shadows and restrained greenery.
- Travertine, limestone, oat-coloured surfaces and subtle natural textures.
- Condensation on cold cans and appetising visible drink layers.
- Coffee beans, ice, vanilla, caramel, chocolate and matcha as appropriate.
- Carefully composed editorial still-life photography.
- Premium but believable product presentation.
- Clean studio product images for e-commerce listings.

Avoid generic coffee-shop stock photography, artificial-looking packaging, excessive props, neon colours, dark industrial styling, crowded compositions and images that misrepresent the actual product.

The website should feel like one coherent photographic campaign rather than a collection of unrelated stock images.

## 9. Agentic SEO and AI-commerce readiness

Treat this as a core architectural requirement, not a marketing add-on.

The objective is to make Coffee Round easy for search engines, AI search systems and authorised shopping agents to discover, understand, compare and accurately represent.

Do not promise inclusion in ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, AI Mode or any other AI-generated result. Implement the technical and content foundations that improve eligibility and machine readability.

### A. Crawlability and discoverability

Ensure:

- Important pages return the correct HTTP status codes.
- Public product and category pages are accessible without login.
- Robots directives do not accidentally block essential pages or resources.
- Canonical URLs are correct.
- Navigation exposes important products and categories through ordinary links.
- Product information is available in rendered, indexable HTML.
- The primary product facts do not exist only inside images or inaccessible JavaScript.
- The XML sitemap includes canonical, indexable URLs.
- The robots.txt file points to the correct sitemap.
- Internal links make all important products discoverable.
- Staging sites are protected from indexing.
- Removed products and discontinued SKUs have deliberate redirect or retirement rules.
- Noindex, canonical and redirect logic is tested after deployment.

Where practical, render core product information and product structured data in the initial HTML rather than relying entirely on delayed client-side JavaScript.

### B. Search-engine and AI crawler access

Review robots.txt, CDN rules, web application firewalls and hosting controls to ensure that legitimate search crawlers are not unintentionally blocked.

Support the relevant crawlers according to the business's goals and the policies of each service.

For OpenAI, distinguish between:

- **OAI-SearchBot**, associated with search discovery.
- **GPTBot**, associated with potential model training.

Configure access deliberately based on Coffee Round's preferences and the current official crawler documentation. Do not assume allowing one requires allowing the other.

Do not blanket-block all bots or allow every bot without consideration. Verify crawler identity using current provider guidance, including IP verification where applicable.

Review the official documentation for Googlebot, Bingbot and relevant commerce platforms.

### C. Product structured data

Implement valid, dynamically generated JSON-LD using the Schema.org vocabulary and the requirements of the relevant search platforms.

At minimum, assess and implement the following where appropriate:

- `Organization` or `OnlineStore`.
- `WebSite`.
- `BreadcrumbList`.
- `Product`.
- `Offer`.
- `ProductGroup` and variant relationships where applicable.
- `Review` and `AggregateRating`, only when supported by genuine reviews.
- `Article` or `BlogPosting` for editorial content.
- `VideoObject` for relevant video pages.
- `PostalAddress` and business details only where verified.
- Merchant return-policy information where supported.

For each purchasable product, include the relevant verified fields:

- Product name.
- Description.
- Brand.
- SKU.
- GTIN, where available.
- Product image URLs.
- Category.
- Offer price and currency.
- Availability.
- Product URL.
- Condition.
- Shipping information where supported.
- Return policy information where supported.
- Genuine review and rating data, if applicable.

For product variants, ensure that size, pack count or other genuine variations are represented correctly.

Prices, stock status, identifiers and shipping details must match the visible website and commerce feed.

Never publish invented GTINs, ratings, review counts or false availability.

Do not add unsupported schema types simply to make the website appear more sophisticated.

Validate the implementation with Google's Rich Results Test, Schema Markup Validator and relevant platform tools. Resolve critical errors and monitor warnings.

### D. Merchant feeds and product discovery

Set up Google Merchant Center and eligible free product listings when the business is ready.

Generate and maintain an accurate product feed containing the required attributes, including:

- Title.

Assess Bing Webmaster Tools and other relevant discovery channels where they provide genuine value.

Do not claim that a feed has been submitted, approved or activated until verified.

### E. Agent-readable product information

Maintain a consistent, structured source of truth for product information across the CMS, storefront, schema markup, merchant feeds, customer-service content and any public product API.

Every product should have a concise, factual, machine-readable description covering:

Design the commerce architecture so future agentic shopping capabilities can be added without rebuilding the entire store.

Assess support for relevant, officially documented protocols and integrations as they mature, including the Universal Commerce Protocol where appropriate.

If a relevant format becomes useful for a defined integration, evaluate it against current official documentation and implement it only when there is a clear purpose.

Prioritise indexability, product accuracy, structured data, useful content, merchant feeds, accessibility and performance first.

### I. AI visibility measurement

## 10. Technical SEO specification

### URL and site architecture

Use short, descriptive, stable URLs.

Examples:

- `/shop/`
- `/products/classic-iced-latte/`
- `/products/vanilla-iced-latte/`
- `/pages/our-story/`
- `/journal/how-to-choose-iced-coffee/`

Avoid unnecessary URL parameters, duplicate category paths, tracking parameters in canonical URLs and arbitrary identifier strings in public slugs.

### Metadata

Every indexable page must have:

- A unique, descriptive title.
- A useful meta description.
- A single clear H1.
- Relevant headings.
- A canonical URL.
- Appropriate indexation directives.
- Social sharing metadata.
- Descriptive internal links.
- Correct language and locale metadata.

Do not force keywords into titles where they make the wording unnatural.

### Keyword research and mapping

Before writing final SEO copy, conduct keyword research for the UK market using available search-volume, search-intent and competitive data.

Develop a keyword map covering:

- Brand and branded-product searches.
- Ready-to-drink iced coffee.
- Iced latte cans.
- Flavoured iced coffee.
- Vanilla iced latte.
- Caramel iced latte.
- Iced mocha.
- Brown sugar iced latte.
- Iced matcha latte.
- Coffee multipacks and discovery boxes.
- Relevant ingredient, caffeine and nutrition questions where product facts support them.

Treat these as seed terms, not guaranteed high-volume opportunities.

For each target, record search intent, estimated demand, competition, target page, content requirements and internal-link opportunities.

Do not invent search volumes, difficulty scores or competitor rankings.

Map each primary commercial keyword group to one appropriate page to avoid competing against the brand's own pages.

### Technical safeguards

Implement:

- XML sitemap generation and validation.
- Robots.txt validation.
- Canonical tags.
- Correct redirects.
- Custom 404 handling.
- Broken-link detection.
- Duplicate metadata detection.
- Pagination handling where needed.
- Image SEO.
- Structured-data validation.
- HTTPS throughout.
- Correct internationalisation signals if multiple markets launch.
- Search Console and webmaster verification.
- Indexation monitoring.

Do not index empty search pages, internal filters, account pages, checkout pages or duplicate tracking URLs.

Do not automatically noindex valuable product pages simply because a filter or tracking parameter exists.

### Internal linking

Link related products, categories, guides and FAQs naturally.

Prioritise:

- Homepage to core products.
- Shop to all purchasable products.
- Guides to relevant product pages.
- Product pages to related products and FAQs.
- Editorial content to relevant categories.
- Breadcrumbs to parent categories.

Use descriptive anchor text and avoid repetitive, exact-match keyword links.

## 14. Accessibility, responsive design and performance

Build for mobile first while delivering an excellent desktop experience.

### Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA as the design and testing standard.

Implement:

- Keyboard navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- Semantic HTML.
- Accessible names for controls.
- Correct heading hierarchy.
- Sufficient text contrast.
- Form labels and helpful error messages.
- Accessible modal and navigation behaviour.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Meaningful image alternatives.
- Screen-reader-friendly basket updates.
- Accessible cookie and consent interfaces.

Do not communicate essential product information through colour alone.

### Performance

Aim for good Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile of real-user visits:

- Largest Contentful Paint: 2.5 seconds or less.
- Interaction to Next Paint: 200 milliseconds or less.
- Cumulative Layout Shift: 0.1 or less.

These are performance targets, not guaranteed results.

Use:

- Optimised, responsive images.
- Appropriate image dimensions.
- Modern image formats.
- Lazy loading below the fold.
- Efficient font loading.
- Minimal third-party scripts.
- Caching and compression.
- Lightweight components.
- Server-side rendering or static rendering where appropriate.
- Stable layouts.
- Fast, accessible navigation.

Test with Lighthouse, PageSpeed Insights and field data when available. Do not claim good real-user Core Web Vitals before sufficient field data exists.
